Understand Climate Change

Climate change is the long-term shift in temperatures and weather patterns. Since the 1800s the main driver has been human activity, mostly burning fossil fuels.

      Climate change and India

      Heatwaves are becoming more frequent and intense across northern India, monsoon rainfall is more erratic, coastal areas face rising seas, and Himalayan glaciers that feed major rivers are retreating. Poor air quality in many cities adds to the health burden.

      India has set a goal of net-zero emissions by 2070 and a target of 500 GW of non-fossil electricity capacity by 2030. Households that save power directly support these goals.

      Smart Buying Guide

      The smart-device trade-off: every connected gadget uses a little standby power (often 1–3 W) and carries manufacturing emissions and e-waste. Buy only what you will really use, start with the biggest energy users like cooling and lighting, and use devices for years.

      The Path to Net Zero

      Buildings account for a large share of global greenhouse gas emissions. Smart, connected devices offer an immediate, actionable fix.

      By automating climate control, lighting and standby power, a household can avoid several hundred kilograms of CO₂ every year.

      Why Every Half-Degree Matters

      IPCC findings comparing 1.5°C and 2°C of warming:

      Coral reefs

      Lose roughly 70–90% at 1.5°C, but more than 99% at 2°C.

      Arctic summer ice

      Ice-free about once a century at 1.5°C, at least once a decade at 2°C.

      Extreme heat

      Hundreds of millions more people face severe heat exposure at 2°C.

      Figures are rounded; see IPCC, NASA and NOAA for current data.
